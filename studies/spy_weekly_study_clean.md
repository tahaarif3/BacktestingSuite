# SPY Weekly-Strategy Study — $25/week variations (2005–2024)

*SPY only · long-only · every scheme contributes the same **$25/week ($1,300/yr)**, so this isolates strategy rather than dollars.*

**Baseline (WA1):** $25/wk bought every Monday → **$106,735**, IRR **12.58%**, max drawdown **−38.10%**.

> Correction from the earlier study: $25/wk looked like the "winner" there only because it invested $1,300/yr vs $1,200/yr elsewhere. On IRR it was actually tied. Here everything is $1,300/yr — apples to apples.

## Verdict

1. **Day-of-week timing is noise.** Monday, Wednesday, and Friday land within a couple hundredths of a percent. Even buying the week's *cheapest* day with perfect hindsight (WA4) only makes 12.67% — **+0.09%** over Monday. The theoretical ceiling on weekly timing is basically zero.
2. **Weekly vs monthly at equal dollars is a tie.** WE2 ($108.33/mo) ≈ WA1 ($25/wk). Contribution frequency doesn't matter.
3. **Only risk levers move the needle.** The trend filter plus cash yield (WB1–WB3) shave ~4–5 points off max drawdown at roughly flat return. Nothing here clears +1% IRR.
4. **Cutting drawdown hard costs real return.** WD1 nearly halves max drawdown (−17.28% vs −38.10%) but gives up 1.76% of IRR to do it.

**Conclusion:** there is no $25/week timing trick that adds ~1%. To out-earn the baseline you must contribute *more dollars*, not time a fixed contribution more cleverly. Where weekly bankroll management genuinely pays off is drawdown — a light 200-SMA filter with idle-cash yield (WB3) roughly matches baseline return with a noticeably calmer ride.
